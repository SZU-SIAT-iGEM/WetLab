INP表面展示工程菌CMC降解能力定性实验

### 二、实验简要流程

1. 菌悬液制备：LB纯化单菌落用M9培养液重悬；
2. 平板接种：7 cm CMC采用点样接种，在平板中心处接种10 µL菌液，37 ℃倒置培养；每隔24小时记录菌落生长情况
3. 染色脱色（避光）：染色前统一黑色背景拍照；每板添加2–3 mL刚果红避光染色30 min，1 mol/L NaCl避光脱色10–15 min；脱色后分别用白色、黑色背景拍照留存；
4. 对照设置：设置MG1665以及DH5a空载体阴性对照、野生型空白对照。

### 四、结果分析

- 本次 CMC 平板筛选体系存在明显的“抗生素与载体不匹配”问题；链霉素对大肠杆菌的筛选效应有限；
- Gibson 组装可能存在连接失败或重组质粒构建失败，导致真正的载体重组体没有被筛选出来；
- 空质粒能够长菌落，是因为空载体保留了正确的复制和抗性功能，营养来源可能是M9培养基残余；而重组载体没有长菌落，则提示该质粒可能在组装或筛选环节失效，而非菌株本身不能生长。

### 五、下一步计划

为进一步验证并优化实验流程，后续实验将围绕“质粒筛选体系、载体构建与验证、转化筛选流程”三部分进行改进。

1. 修正筛选体系，确保抗性基因有筛选效果
2. 重新梳理质粒构建逻辑，确认载体类型和目的基因插入方式
   - 明确目的基因插入位点和表达框架，确保与载体兼容，避免构建后因载体失效或复制缺失导致无菌落。
3. 优化 Gibson 组装流程，确保重组质粒正确构建
   - 在后续实验中，先对目的基因进行 PCR 扩增，并通过琼脂糖凝胶验证扩增产物大小和纯度。
   - 对线性化载体进行检测，确认骨架完整且无错误切割。
   - Gibson 组装完成后，进行 PCR 或酶切验证，确认插入片段与载体连接正确，再进行转化。
   - 若组装效果不理想，应检查引物同源臂设计、模板质量、片段浓度和反应比例等参数。
4. 强化转化前后验证，避免“空质粒正常生长但重组质粒无菌落”的误判
   - 设置空载体对照、重组质粒对照、无DNA对照等多个对照组。
   - 对转化后菌落数、大小和生长情况进行记录，判断是否存在转化效率低或筛选条件不匹配问题。
   - 若空载体正常生长而重组载体无菌落，应优先考虑筛选体系不匹配或组装失败，而非简单归因于细胞无法生长。
5. 后续采用标准流程：目的基因 PCR → 产物验证 → Gibson 组装 → 组装产物鉴定 → 转化 → 抗生素筛选 → 单克隆筛选 → 进一步 CMC 质粒功能验证。

   

   

   
   

# CMC‑Congo Red Plate Qualitative Screening Second Experiment Record

## I. Basic Experimental Information

Qualitative experiment on CMC‑degrading ability of INP surface‑display engineering strains

Experiment date: 2026‑08‑21 — 2026‑08‑25 Experimental strains: INP‑Cel5L, INP‑Cel48S, INP‑Cel9K Experiment type: Qualitative experiment

Supplementary note: Improvements were made to the second experiment based on lessons learned from the first trial. Optimizations include: pouring plates uniformly to guarantee consistent agar thickness; extending incubation time to 96 hours; adopting the spot‑inoculation method for plate inoculation according to CMC‑Congo red qualitative protocols from other research groups.

## II. Brief Experimental Procedure

1. Preparation of bacterial suspension: Purified single colonies from LB medium were resuspended in M9 medium.
2. Plate inoculation: Spot‑inoculation was performed on 7 cm CMC plates. 10 µL bacterial suspension was spotted at the center of each plate, followed by static incubation at 37 °C in an inverted position. Colony growth was recorded every 24 hours.
3. Staining and destaining (protected from light): Photographs were taken against a black background prior to staining. Each plate was added with 2‑3 mL Congo red solution for 30‑minute staining in darkness, then destained with 1 mol/L NaCl for 10‑15 minutes under light‑shielded conditions. Images were captured against both white and black backgrounds after destaining for documentation.
4. Control setup: MG1665 and DH5α empty‑vector negative controls, as well as wild‑type blank control were included.

1. Colony growth

- 24 h incubation: Only MG1665‑EV formed distinct visible colonies; faint colonies of Cel5L were observed, while almost no bacterial growth was visible for other groups. No contamination was detected in blank areas of all plates.
- 48 h incubation: Clear colonies were maintained for MG1665‑EV; other groups showed negligible changes. Blank regions remained uncontaminated.

1. Staining performance Plates exhibited bright‑red background overall. No large, well‑defined transparent hydrolytic zones were formed. Cel‑series strains barely grew. Neither blank control nor negative control showed color alteration.

## IV. Result Analysis

- Obvious mismatch between antibiotics and vector was identified in the present CMC plate screening system; streptomycin provided insufficient selective pressure for *Escherichia coli*.
- Gibson assembly may have failed to generate correct ligation products or recombinant plasmids, so genuine recombinant transformants were not obtained during screening.
- Colonies formed from empty plasmids because empty vectors retained intact replication and antibiotic‑resistance functions. Residual nutrients in M9 medium may support their growth. By contrast, failure of recombinant vectors to yield colonies suggests defects during assembly or screening steps rather than intrinsic growth defects of host strains.

## V. Future Plans

Further validation and procedural optimization will be carried out focusing on three aspects: plasmid screening system, vector construction & verification, and transformation‑screening workflow.

1. Revise the screening system to ensure effective selection exerted by resistance markers.
2. Re‑examine plasmid‑construction logic; confirm vector identity and insertion pattern of target genes
   - Clarify insertion sites and expression cassettes of target genes to ensure compatibility with the vector, avoiding vector dysfunction or replication loss after construction which leads to absence of colonies.
3. Optimize Gibson assembly workflow to obtain correct recombinant plasmids
   - Perform PCR amplification of target genes and verify amplicon size and purity via agarose‑gel electrophoresis before assembly.
   - Examine linearized vectors to confirm backbone integrity and correct restriction digestion.
   - After Gibson assembly, verify insert‑vector ligation by PCR or restriction digestion prior to transformation.
   - If assembly performance is unsatisfactory, check parameters including primer homology‑arm design, template quality, fragment concentration and reaction stoichiometry.
4. Strengthen pre‑ and post‑transformation validation to avoid misinterpretation caused by “normal growth of empty vector yet no colonies from recombinant plasmids”
   - Set multiple controls: empty‑vector control, recombinant‑plasmid control and no‑DNA control.
   - Record transformant counts, colony size and growth status to evaluate low transformation efficiency or mismatched screening conditions.
   - When empty vectors grow normally whereas recombinant constructs produce no colonies, prioritize troubleshooting screening‑system incompatibility or assembly failure instead of attributing the phenomenon solely to host‑cell growth incapability.
5. Adopt standardized workflow for subsequent experiments: target‑gene PCR → product validation → Gibson assembly → assembly‑product characterization → transformation → antibiotic‑based selection → monoclonal screening → further functional validation of CMC‑degrading plasmids.
